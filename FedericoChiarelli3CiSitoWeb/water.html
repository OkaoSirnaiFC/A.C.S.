Water: The Phlegm

Water was one of the classical elements in ancient Greek philosophy, as well as in Indian and Chinese traditional systems, and was commonly associated with emotion and intuition. Among the early Greek thinkers, Empedocles established water as one of the four fundamental elements alongside air, fire, and earth. Plato associated water with the icosahedron, a geometric solid with many sides, which he believed reflected water’s flowing nature. Later, Aristotle described water as cold and wet and placed it between air and earth within the structure of the cosmos. In ancient medicine, water was linked to phlegm, the phlegmatic temperament, winter, femininity, and the western direction. In alchemy, water was represented by a downward-pointing triangle and commonly associated with mercury.

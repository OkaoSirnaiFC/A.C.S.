Air: The Blood

Air, or wind, was one of the four classical elements in ancient Greek philosophy and Western alchemy, associated with life, movement, and intelligence. Anaximenes believed air was the fundamental substance of the universe, while Diogenes of Apollonia linked it to the soul and human intelligence. Plato associated air with the octahedron and described it as hot and wet. The Greeks distinguished between aer, the lower atmosphere, and aether, the pure upper air considered celestial. In ancient medicine, air was connected to blood and the sanguine temperament, symbolizing vitality and sociability. These ideas became widely known and were even mocked by Aristophanes in The Clouds. In alchemy and later traditions, air came to represent intellect, communication, and spiritual freedom.

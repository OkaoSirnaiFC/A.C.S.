Gold

The Metal of the Sun

Gold was associated with the Sun in nearly every alchemical and astrological tradition. Its incorruptibility, brilliance, and rarity made it the supreme symbol of perfection, immortality, and divine authority. Alchemists believed gold embodied spiritual completion and the perfected state of matter. In Hermetic philosophy, it represented illumination, vitality, kingship, and the eternal masculine force radiating through creation.

☽

Silver

The Metal of the Moon

Silver belonged to the Moon and symbolized reflection, intuition, dreams, and purity. Ancient mystics viewed silver as receptive and fluid, mirroring the shifting nature of lunar cycles and the subconscious mind. In alchemy, silver represented the soul in its purified form and was often paired with gold as the union of lunar and solar principles.

Iron

The Metal of Mars

Iron was linked to Mars, the planet of war, force, courage, and bloodshed. Weapons forged from iron became symbols of conquest and masculine aggression throughout the ancient world. Alchemists associated iron with raw strength, conflict, endurance, and the transformative power that emerges through struggle and fire.

♀

Copper

The Metal of Venus

Copper was sacred to Venus, embodying beauty, sensuality, harmony, and artistic creation. Its warm glow and conductive nature made it a symbol of attraction and emotional connection. In esoteric traditions, copper represented love, fertility, pleasure, and the magnetic forces that bind both people and the cosmos together.

♃

Tin

The Metal of Jupiter

Tin corresponded with Jupiter, ruler of expansion, wisdom, prosperity, and divine law. Alchemists viewed tin as generous and noble, associated with abundance, philosophy, and spiritual authority. Jupiter’s influence was believed to inspire optimism, justice, and higher understanding across both earthly and celestial realms.

♄

Lead

The Metal of Saturn

Lead was the metal of Saturn, representing time, mortality, discipline, and decay. Though heavy and dull, lead held immense symbolic importance in alchemy because it represented the beginning of transformation. The transmutation of lead into gold became a metaphor for spiritual awakening, mastery, and the refinement of the human soul.

QuicksilverS

The Metal of Mercury

Quicksilver (or mercury), the only liquid metal known to the ancients, was associated with motion, intellect, language, and transformation. Alchemists considered mercury the bridge between spirit and matter: volatile, mysterious, and endlessly changing. It symbolized duality itself: life and death, masculine and feminine, heaven and earth united in one shifting substance.
